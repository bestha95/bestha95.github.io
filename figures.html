My research combines observational studies of exoplanet atmospheres with the development and characterization of astronomical instruments. The work presented here includes high-resolution transmission spectroscopy, instrumental systematics, atmospheric dispersion measurements, and instrumentation concepts for future large telescopes.

Broadband Atmospheric Dispersion Corrector for HROS–TMT

Designed and analysed a broadband Atmospheric Dispersion Corrector for the High Resolution Optical Spectrograph planned for the Thirty Meter Telescope. The design aims to minimize atmospheric dispersion over a broad wavelength range while maintaining high optical performance.

Why it matters: Atmospheric dispersion can introduce wavelength-dependent image shifts and reduce the efficiency and precision of spectroscopic observations. Correcting it is particularly important for high-resolution spectroscopy and future Extremely Large Telescopes.

Astronomical Instrumentation · Optical Design · TMT

Probing the Atmosphere of KELT-9 b

High-resolution transmission spectroscopy of the ultra-hot Jupiter KELT-9 b using observations from the Himalayan Chandra Telescope. The analysis focuses on detecting atmospheric species and modelling stellar effects that can influence the planetary transmission signal.

Why it matters: Ultra-hot Jupiters provide laboratories for studying atmospheric chemistry under extreme temperatures. High-resolution spectroscopy allows individual atomic species and their Doppler signatures to be investigated.

Exoplanet Atmospheres · High-Resolution Spectroscopy · HCT

Understanding Instrumental Systematics in Transmission Spectroscopy

Investigating wavelength-dependent and time-dependent systematics that affect ground-based multi-object transmission spectroscopy. The work examines how instrumental behaviour, spectral trace variations, and correlated noise can propagate into atmospheric measurements.

Why it matters: Small instrumental effects can sometimes mimic or distort planetary atmospheric signals. Understanding these systematics is essential for improving the reliability and precision of ground-based exoplanet characterization.

Data Analysis · Gaussian Processes · Transmission Spectroscopy

Atmospheric Dispersion Measurements at Hanle

Measurement and characterization of atmospheric dispersion using observations obtained with the Hanle Faint Object Spectrograph and Camera mounted on the Himalayan Chandra Telescope.

Why it matters: Direct measurements provide an observational understanding of how atmospheric dispersion affects spectroscopic observations at the site. These results are useful for improving observing strategies and achieving better spectrophotometric precision.

Observatory Characterization · Atmospheric Dispersion · HFOSC

Multi-Object Spectroscopy and Fiber Positioning for Future Telescopes

Developed concepts and simulations for multi-object spectroscopic observations, including a multi-degree-of-freedom fiber positioning system and observing strategies for high-resolution spectroscopy.

Why it matters: Multi-object spectroscopy can improve observing efficiency and provide simultaneous reference measurements for mitigating observational systematics. Fiber-positioning systems are therefore important components of future highly multiplexed astronomical instruments.
