About

I am a doctoral researcher at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, Bengaluru, working in the field of astronomical instrumentation and exoplanet atmospheric characterisation.

My research combines observational astronomy, astronomical instrumentation, and data analysis to improve our ability to study the atmospheres of planets beyond our Solar System. I am particularly interested in ground-based transmission spectroscopy and the challenges associated with achieving high-precision measurements.

My work involves both low- and high-resolution spectroscopy, together with the development and evaluation of observational strategies, correction of stellar and instrumental systematics, and the design of instrumentation for future astronomical facilities.

Research Interests

My primary research interests are focused on the spectroscopic characterisation of exoplanet atmospheres using ground-based observations. I work with low- and high-resolution transmission spectroscopy and investigate the effects of stellar, telluric, and instrumental systematics on atmospheric measurements.

Alongside exoplanet science, I work on astronomical instrumentation, including optical design and simulation, atmospheric dispersion correction, and fiber-positioning concepts for multi-object spectrographs and future large telescopes.

Current Focus

My current work explores the precision limits of ground-based transmission spectroscopy and the observational and instrumental strategies required for reliable exoplanet atmosphere measurements. I am also interested in connecting instrumentation development with the scientific requirements of future exoplanet observations.
