Hi there! 👋

I am an astronomer working at the intersection of astronomical instrumentation and exoplanet science, with a particular interest in understanding planetary atmospheres through ground-based spectroscopy.

My research explores how observations from ground-based telescopes can reveal the physical and chemical properties of distant exoplanets. Alongside observational astronomy, I work on the design and simulation of astronomical instruments, bringing together optical engineering, mechanical design, and data analysis.

I completed the Integrated M.Tech–PhD program in Astronomical Instrumentation at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics (IIA), Bengaluru, and have submitted my PhD thesis to the University of Calcutta. I am currently awaiting my thesis defense.

Research Interests

Explore my primary research areas:

My research focuses on ground-based low- and high-resolution transmission spectroscopy of exoplanet atmospheres, particularly ultra-hot Jupiters such as KELT-9 b and other transiting systems. I use observations from ground-based facilities, including the 2 m Himalayan Chandra Telescope (HCT), to investigate planetary atmospheric properties and to develop observational techniques for precise atmospheric characterization.

View Research Highlights →

I specialize in the design and simulation of astronomical instruments. This includes optical design of broadband Atmospheric Dispersion Correctors (ADCs) for the High-Resolution Optical Spectrograph on the Thirty Meter Telescope (HROS-TMT) and mechanical design of multi-functional fiber positioning systems for extremely large telescopes.

Explore TMT & Fiber Designs →

I study the observational systematics that affect ground-based exoplanet transmission spectroscopy, focusing on elevation-dependent effects, instrumental flexure, and atmospheric refraction. By characterizing and correcting these sources of variability, I aim to improve the precision and reliability of planetary atmospheric measurements.
